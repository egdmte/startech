August 05, 2026: Then something unexpected. After adding some counters to see what the steering was actually doing, we ran it - and found what is probably the biggest fault in the whole project, bigger than anything we found by reading. The car measures how fast the road position is changing, in pixels PER SECOND, but the two numbers it compares that against were clearly chosen as pixels PER FRAME. At 30 pictures a second, the road only has to shift 1.7 pixels between two pictures - about half a percent of a lane width - for the car to slam down to its slowest speed AND start spinning on the spot. With the lane in perfect view.

August 05, 2026: Measured it properly rather than guessing: with about 2 pixels of normal camera wobble, 59 percent of frames end up spinning the car. August 05, 2026: Scaling them all down does not work either. The motors simply do not turn below 30 percent, and that is a fact about the motors, not about the battery - it does not shrink when you lower the voltage. Scale everything down and the slowest speed becomes 17, which means "not moving". Worse, our slowest speed is ALREADY 25, which is already below 30. Those two numbers have been arguing with each other since before the competition.

August 05, 2026: And the real cost, which nobody had worked out: today the car has a usable range from 30 to 85. With a 57 ceiling it would be 30 to 57. That is half the steering resolution gone, and the half that remains sits right on top of the range where the motors behave worst. Written into the plan with the conclusion: measure the actual voltage and the actual dead zone FIRST, then choose all the speeds together as one set.
